# How the timeline works

Why one life event appears on several pages, and what the timeline shows in what order.

## One event, seen from every person in it

When Monica and Chandler married, their friends were there. In Monica, the wedding is one life event, with Monica, Chandler and each guest listed under **Who was there**. It is not copied onto each page: every person’s timeline reads the same event.

This is deliberate. We think that recording a fact once should fill in both the day it happened and the records of the people involved, and that you should never have to enter the same thing twice. So:

- You add the wedding once, from any of the pages of the people in it.
- A change you make from Chandler’s page shows on Rachel’s page too.
- Deleting it from one page takes it off every page.

The entry says who else was there. **With Monica, Ross, Rachel** lists the other people. Each name leads to that person’s page, except for a partial contact, who has no place in your list of people.

## Where the timeline is

Each contact’s timeline is on the **Overview** tab of their page, under **Timeline**. It holds their life events, and nothing else: relationships, reminders and notes have their own places on the page.

## What order it is in

The newest event comes first. Events are grouped by year, and each year says how many entries it holds. Within a year, the most recent date comes first.

The timeline shows a page of entries at a time. At the bottom, choose **Load more** to go further back.

## What each entry shows

An entry shows the type of event or its summary, its date, and the details you chose to fill in, such as the place, the description and the fields of its type. Each type has its own icon and colour at the start of the line, so a move and a wedding look different at a glance.

Two short labels help you find your own changes: **Just added** marks an event you have just recorded, and **Edited** one you have just changed.

## Life events and relationships

A relationship and a life event record different things. That Ross and Rachel are partners is a relationship. That they moved in together on a given day is a life event. Adding, changing or ending a relationship does not put anything on the timeline, so record the day itself as a life event if you want to remember it.
